\section{Why this exists}
Enterprises are moving from experiments to \textbf{agents that act}: they spend budget, access data, invoke tools, and change customer-facing state. The hard gap is \textbf{runtime control}---who approved what ran, and \textbf{what you can prove} afterward to finance, security, and auditors.

\textbf{Connector} is a self-hosted \textbf{governance and execution layer} between agents and production: policy, budgets, traces, and receipts at the action boundary---not a chat product or ``prompt tuning'' alone.

\section{Capabilities}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mediates \textbf{tool calls and side effects} so nothing bypasses policy silently at the runtime boundary.
  \item Ties \textbf{cost and risk} to decisions and traces, not only aggregate vendor spend.
  \item \textbf{Deploys beside} existing stacks---control at the edge first; no full toolchain rip-and-replace on day one.
\end{itemize}

\section{Architecture \& stage}
\textbf{Stack:} agent OS-style layers (memory and packets at a boundary; action authorization; governed compile/execute). We focus the \textbf{write path}---where agents commit state, spend, and obligation---with deterministic operational discipline around model steps, bounded by policy and recorded as proof.

\textbf{Controlled beta:} runtime and workflows in self-hosted settings; design partners under real agent, tool, and compliance pressure. We understate distribution; we stand behind a \textbf{clear thesis and an implementation aligned to it}.
