\chapter{Executive Summary}

\section{What Connector is (one sentence)}

\textbf{Connector OS} is a \textbf{self-hosted governance node} for AI agents and workflows: it sits between your agents and production systems and makes every consequential action \textbf{isolatable}, \textbf{policy-bound at runtime}, and \textbf{provable afterward}.

\section{What we are not}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Not a chatbot or copilot product.
  \item Not ``prompt tuning'' or guardrails-only filtering.
  \item Not a replacement for your entire AI stack on day one.
  \item Not a giant transformation program sold as the first conversation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Product shape: one node, many workloads}

Customers install \textbf{one Connector OS node} (\texttt{connector-platform} + \texttt{connectorctl}). On that node they run:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{First-party plugins} (DevGuard, TraceTramp, WitnessCtl, LedgerLens, and planned apps).
  \item \textbf{Custom workflows} (CLS / company-specific automation authored for their business).
  \item \textbf{Third-party AGOS plugins} (packaged \texttt{.cpkg} workloads from a hub model).
\end{itemize}

Think \textbf{operating system + applications}, not a bag of disconnected microservices.

\section{Entry wedge (how we sell in 2026)}

We enter through \textbf{one live workflow} where AI already takes action and mistakes hurt:

\begin{itemize}
  \item support automation (refunds, routing, discounts),
  \item fintech ops (flags, reviews, exceptions),
  \item internal automation (approvals, case routing).
\end{itemize}

We sell \textbf{decision trace + explanation + compliance-ready output} for that workflow---not ``the whole platform vision'' in meeting one.

\section{Stage}

\textbf{Controlled beta.} Runtime, plugins, and workflows are exercised in self-hosted and lab settings with design partners. Position honestly: thesis is clear; distribution is founder-led; proof comes from narrow pilots.
