\section{What changed from the broad list}
This version is intentionally stricter than the original 100-company universe. It is optimized for current founder-led reality, not theoretical market size.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kept}: companies with strong workflow pain, visible AI execution complexity, likely pilot budget, and a believable path to an early collaboration.
\item \textbf{Removed or de-emphasized}: hyperscalers, frontier labs, very large incumbents, and companies likely to be too slow, too political, or too internally capable for an early design-partner motion.
\item \textbf{Preferred}: growth-stage or focused enterprise startups with one clear workflow surface, one likely buyer, and a bounded pilot that can prove value quickly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ground-reality filter}
A company stays on this v2 list only if most of the following are true:
\begin{itemize}
\item The pain is already live in production or close to production.
\item There is a plausible budget owner for a small paid pilot.
\item The workflow can be bounded to one concrete use case.
\item The buyer is likely an engineering, platform, operations, or product owner with enough urgency to try an early collaboration.
\item The company is not so large or mature that it will default to building internally or burying the motion in procurement.
\end{itemize}

\section{Executive summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ICP1 total}: 50 companies.
\item \textbf{ICP2 total}: 30 companies.
\item \textbf{ICP3 total}: 20 companies.
\item \textbf{Best immediate wedge}: support automation, enterprise work AI, contact center AI, AI workflow tooling, and regulated workflow automation with high audit pressure.
\item \textbf{Best initial buyer shape}: VP Engineering, Head of AI, Head of Support Technology, Head of Risk Platform, Legal Ops leader, or healthcare operations / product leader.
\end{itemize}
