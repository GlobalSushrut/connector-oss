\chapter{Our Entry Wedge}

The wedge is the most important part of this document.

We are not entering the market as a broad AI platform.
We are entering through a narrow and concrete use case:

\begin{conceptbox}{Entry wedge}
\centering
\Large\bfseries AI-driven workflows where decisions matter.
\end{conceptbox}

This means workflows where AI is not just speaking, but deciding or acting in ways that affect money, customers, approvals, or operations.

\section{Where this wedge shows up}

The clearest early examples are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item support automation,
  \item fintech ops,
  \item internal copilots and internal automation.
\end{itemize}

These are good entry points because the decision has consequences.
Someone feels the pain when the AI gets it wrong.
And the value of a better control layer can be understood quickly.

\section{What we sell}

We sell:

\begin{insightbox}{What we sell first}
\centering
\Large\bfseries decision trace + explanation + compliance-ready output
\end{insightbox}

In practical terms, that means for one workflow we help the customer answer:

\begin{itemize}
  \item what the AI did,
  \item why it did it,
  \item what path or context led to that output,
  \item what evidence can be shown to internal or external stakeholders.
\end{itemize}

This is the story in buyer language.
We are not leading with models, orchestration, protocols, or internal architecture.
We are leading with clarity and proof around one risky workflow.

\section{What we do not sell}

We are not selling:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a full platform replacement,
  \item all AI infrastructure,
  \item every engine we have built,
  \item a giant transformation program,
  \item a heavy integration project.
\end{itemize}

This is important because the fastest way to lose momentum is to make Connector feel too large.

\begin{warnbox}{What the first conversation is not}
Do not lead with: ``Here is the whole Connector platform.''

Lead with: ``Pick one workflow where your AI is making decisions. We will make it traceable, explainable, and provable.''
\end{warnbox}

That is easier to understand, easier to buy, and easier to champion internally.
