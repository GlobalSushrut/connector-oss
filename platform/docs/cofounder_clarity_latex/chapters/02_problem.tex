\chapter{The Problem We Are Entering Through}

AI is moving from chat to action.

It is no longer just answering questions or drafting text.
It is now being used to:

\begin{itemize}
  \item issue refunds,
  \item route support cases,
  \item approve or block transactions,
  \item update records,
  \item trigger internal workflows,
  \item make recommendations that directly affect customers or money.
\end{itemize}

That changes the problem.

When AI only drafts text, mistakes are mostly annoying.
When AI starts taking action, mistakes become operational.
They affect revenue, trust, escalations, internal confidence, and sometimes compliance.

\section{What teams cannot answer today}

When something goes wrong, most teams struggle to answer three basic questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item What happened?
  \item Why did it happen?
  \item Can we prove it?
\end{enumerate}

This sounds simple, but it is where the pain lives.

A support team cannot explain why the AI issued a refund.
A fintech team cannot explain why a transaction was flagged.
An internal automation team cannot show why a workflow changed path.

The result is predictable:

\begin{itemize}
  \item automation slows down,
  \item people get pulled into incident review,
  \item buyers lose trust,
  \item security and compliance teams become blockers,
  \item AI projects lose momentum.
\end{itemize}

\begin{dangerbox}{Human version of the problem}
When something goes wrong, teams cannot clearly explain or prove what the AI did.
\end{dangerbox}

\section{Why this is a good entry point}

This is a good market entry problem because it is:

\begin{itemize}
  \item real,
  \item already happening,
  \item easy to understand,
  \item close to business pain,
  \item close to budget.
\end{itemize}

We do not need to educate the market about a future problem.
We need to help teams solve a present one.

That is a much better place to start.
