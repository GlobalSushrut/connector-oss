\chapter{Why This Wedge Exists Now}

This wedge exists now because three things are changing at the same time.

\section{AI is moving from content generation to workflow execution}

Companies are no longer using AI only for drafting or summarization.
They are putting it inside workflows that touch real operations.

That means the question is no longer just ``Does the output look good?''
The question becomes ``Can we trust this inside a real process?''

External market research in 2025 and 2026 points in the same direction:
AI adoption is moving deeper into business workflows, and agentic systems are becoming part of how companies think about operating leverage.

\section{Risk rises as autonomy rises}

As AI becomes more capable, weak control becomes more expensive.
This is not only a security point.
It is an operations point.

The more the system can do, the more a company needs:

\begin{itemize}
  \item oversight,
  \item accountability,
  \item clear ownership,
  \item repeatable controls,
  \item evidence when something goes wrong.
\end{itemize}

PwC's 2025 Responsible AI research is useful here because it shows governance moving closer to the teams that build and run AI.
That is an important signal.
Governance is not staying a policy deck.
It is moving into operations.

\section{Governance pressure is real, not theoretical}

The European Commission's AI Act timeline makes the pressure concrete.
The Act is already in force, with obligations applying in phases and full applicability scheduled for August 2026.

Even outside Europe, the practical effect is larger than the law itself:

\begin{itemize}
  \item customers ask harder questions,
  \item legal teams become more involved,
  \item compliance becomes part of vendor review,
  \item proof matters more than good intentions.
\end{itemize}

\begin{conceptbox}{Simple market logic}
Output is not enough.
Proof is required.
\end{conceptbox}

\section{Why this matters for Connector}

Connector fits this moment because it is not just helping AI produce outcomes.
It is helping companies understand, defend, and govern those outcomes.

That is why the wedge is timely.
It sits exactly where adoption and accountability now meet.
