Compliance frameworks AI agents must satisfy

SOC 2

AI agents that access customer data create new CC6 and CC7 control requirements. Connector's HMAC-chained audit journal satisfies these controls with tamper-evident evidence.

HIPAA

Agents that process or retrieve PHI must have access controls, audit logs, and minimum necessary data enforcement. Connector enforces all three at the execution layer.

EU AI Act

High-risk AI systems require human oversight, decision logging, and audit trails (Article 12, 14). Connector produces the record-keeping and HITL infrastructure the Act demands.

GDPR

Agents that access personal data must enforce data minimization and purpose limitation. Connector's namespace isolation and selective context construction enforce both.

What AI agent compliance actually requires

  • Pre-execution policy checks — not post-hoc monitoring
  • Tamper-evident audit trails tied to specific agent actions
  • Data access controls enforced at the execution layer
  • Human-in-the-loop escalation paths for high-risk decisions
  • Evidence bundles that map to specific compliance controls
  • Proof of policy version active at time of each decision

Why traditional compliance tools don't cover AI agents

Logs aren't receipts

Traditional audit logs are mutable. Compliance reviewers increasingly require tamper-evident receipts — cryptographic proof that a log entry hasn't been altered after the fact.

Monitoring isn't enforcement

Observability tools tell you what happened. Compliance requires enforcement — blocking non-compliant actions before they execute, not alerting on them afterward.

Agents change the control surface

Traditional controls assume human users. AI agents can act at machine speed, across multiple systems, simultaneously. The control surface is fundamentally different.
